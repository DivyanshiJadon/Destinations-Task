About Kyoto

Kyoto, the former imperial capital of Japan, is a city where ancient traditions are preserved amidst modern life. Known as the cultural heart of Japan, Kyoto offers a glimpse into traditional Japan with its thousands of temples, shrines, and traditional gardens.

The city was largely spared from bombing during World War II, allowing its rich cultural heritage to remain intact. From the golden pavilion of Kinkaku-ji to the thousands of vermillion torii gates at Fushimi Inari Shrine, Kyoto's landmarks are breathtakingly beautiful.

Beyond its famous temples, Kyoto is known for traditional arts, including tea ceremonies, flower arranging, and geisha culture in the historic Gion district. The city's seasonal beauty is celebrated throughout the year, with cherry blossoms in spring, lush greenery in summer, colorful foliage in fall, and serene snowscapes in winter.

Whether you're meditating in Zen gardens, participating in a tea ceremony, strolling through bamboo forests, or simply enjoying traditional kaiseki cuisine, Kyoto offers an authentic Japanese experience that connects visitors to centuries of tradition.

Top Attractions in Tokyo

Fushimi Inari Shrine

Famous shrine with thousands of vermillion torii gates winding up a mountainside, dedicated to the god of rice and prosperity.

Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion)

Zen temple covered in gold leaf, reflected in a tranquil pond, creating one of Kyoto's most iconic sights.

Arashiyama Bamboo Grove

Enchanting forest of towering bamboo creating a serene atmosphere perfect for peaceful walks.

Kiyomizu-dera

Historic temple built on a hillside with a wooden stage offering panoramic views of the city.

Gion District

Historic geisha district with traditional wooden machiya houses, teahouses, and the possibility of spotting geisha.

Philosopher's Path

Scenic stone path following a canal lined with hundreds of cherry trees, beautiful during sakura season.

Must Try Cuisine in Kyoto

Kaiseki

Traditional multi-course Japanese dinner emphasizing seasonality, artful presentation, and refined flavors.

Yudofu

Simmered tofu, a Kyoto specialty, served in a light kombu broth with seasonal garnishes.

Matcha

Finely ground green tea powder used in traditional tea ceremonies and modern desserts and drinks.

Yatsuhashi

Sweet mochi triangles filled with red bean paste, often wrapped in bamboo leaf, a popular Kyoto souvenir.

Best Time to Visit

Spring

March - May (10-20°C)

Cherry blossom season (late March to early April) is magical but crowded. Mild weather perfect for temple visits.

Summer

June - August (22-33°C)

Hot and humid with rainy season in June. Fewer tourists but can be uncomfortable for outdoor activities.

Fall

September - November (12-22°C)

Best time to visit with comfortable temperatures, beautiful fall foliage, and fewer crowds than spring.

Winter

December - February (0-8°C)

Cold with occasional snow. Serene atmosphere at temples with fewer tourists. Great for hot springs.

Travel Tips & Cost Estimate

Travel Tips

  • Transportation

    Kyoto's bus system is extensive but can be confusing. Consider getting a bus pass or using the subway for major routes.

  • Temple Etiquette

    Dress modestly when visiting temples. Remove shoes before entering temple buildings and be respectful of worshippers.

  • Temple Hours

    Many temples close by 5pm. Plan your day accordingly and check opening hours in advance.

  • Geisha Spotting

    Respect geisha and maiko. Do not block their path or chase them for photos. Evening is best for sightings in Gion.
