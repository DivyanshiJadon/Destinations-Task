About Machu Picchu

Machu Picchu, the Lost City of the Incas, is one of the world's most awe-inspiring archaeological sites. Perched high in the Andes Mountains, this 15th-century Inca citadel showcases the architectural and engineering prowess of one of history's most fascinating civilizations.

Discovered by American historian Hiram Bingham in 1911, Machu Picchu was hidden from Spanish conquistadors and remained largely intact. The site's precise stone constructions, astronomical alignments, and stunning mountain setting create a mystical atmosphere that captivates all who visit.

While Machu Picchu itself is the main draw, the journey there often includes exploring the Sacred Valley and the historic city of Cusco, which served as the capital of the Inca Empire. The entire region is rich with ancient history, colorful markets, and stunning Andean scenery.

Visiting Machu Picchu is a profoundly spiritual and awe-inspiring experience, a testament to human ingenuity and the majesty of the natural world.

Top Attractions in Machu Picchu

The Citadel

The iconic Inca site featuring temples, plazas, and residential areas showcasing Inca stonework and urban planning.

Huayna Picchu

Iconic peak overlooking Machu Picchu with a steep hiking trail offering breathtaking views and access to the Temple of the Moon.

Sun Gate

Inti Punku, the original entrance to Machu Picchu from the Inca Trail, offering a classic view of the entire site.

Inca Bridge

Secret trail built by the Incas as a hidden entrance to the city, consisting of a narrow path carved into a cliff face.

Temple of the Sun

Semi-circular temple aligned with astronomical events, featuring some of the finest stonework at Machu Picchu.

Sacred Valley

Fertile valley along the Urubamba River with traditional markets, Inca ruins, and picturesque villages en route to Machu Picchu.

Must Try Cuisine in Machu Picchu

Ceviche

Fresh raw fish cured in citrus juices and spiced with ají peppers, often served with sweet potato and corn.

Lomo Saltado

Stir-fried beef with onions, tomatoes, and French fries, a flavorful fusion of Peruvian and Asian cuisine.

Aji de Gallina

Creamy chicken stew made with aji amarillo peppers, bread, milk, and walnuts, offering a unique flavor profile.

Quinoa Soup

Nutritious and hearty soup made with quinoa, vegetables, and herbs, perfect for acclimating to high altitudes.

Best Time to Visit

Spring

September - November (18-25°C)

Dry season with pleasant temperatures. Ideal time to visit with clear skies and good hiking conditions.

Summer

December - February (20-28°C)

Rainy season with lush landscapes but potential trail closures. Fewer tourists and lower prices.

Fall

March - May (18-25°C)

Transition from rainy to dry season. Good balance of fewer crowds and generally favorable weather.

Winter

June - August (15-22°C)

Dry season (peak tourist season). Sunny days but cold nights. Best for hiking and clear views.

Travel Tips & Cost Estimate

Travel Tips

  • Book in Advance

    Machu Picchu tickets and train tickets sell out months in advance, especially for high season. Book as early as possible.

  • Altitude Sickness

    Spend time in Cusco or Sacred Valley to acclimate before visiting. Stay hydrated, avoid alcohol, and consider coca tea.

  • Hiking Preparation

    If hiking the Inca Trail, train beforehand and book with a reputable operator. Porters carry most gear but the trail is challenging.

  • Weather Preparedness

    Weather can change quickly. Bring layers, rain gear, and sun protection regardless of season.
