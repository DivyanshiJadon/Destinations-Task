About Reykjavik

Reykjavik, the world's northernmost capital, is a vibrant city where colorful buildings contrast with the dramatic natural landscape of Iceland. This compact city serves as the gateway to exploring Iceland's otherworldly landscapes of glaciers, volcanoes, and geysers.

Despite its small size, Reykjavik packs a cultural punch with its thriving music scene, innovative cuisine, and rich Viking heritage. The city's creative energy is evident in its numerous art galleries, museums, and colorful street art.

Reykjavik's unique position makes it an ideal base for hunting the Northern Lights in winter or enjoying the midnight sun in summer. The city's commitment to sustainability and geothermal energy is reflected in its clean environment and innovative architecture.

Whether you're soaking in the famous Blue Lagoon, exploring volcanic landscapes, whale watching in the North Atlantic, or simply enjoying the city's cozy cafes and restaurants, Reykjavik offers a perfect blend of urban culture and natural wonders.

Top Attractions in Reykjavik

Hallgrímskirkja

Iconic church and Reykjavik's tallest building, designed to resemble volcanic basalt formations, offering panoramic city views from its tower.

Harpa Concert Hall

Stunning glass concert hall with a distinctive honeycomb facade that reflects the city's harbor and mountains, hosting concerts and events.

Blue Lagoon

World-famous geothermal spa with milky blue mineral-rich waters, located in a lava field and known for its healing properties.

Golden Circle

Popular tourist route covering Þingvellir National Park, Geysir geothermal area, and Gullfoss waterfall, showcasing Iceland's natural wonders.

Northern Lights

Natural light display in Earth's sky, best seen in winter months on clear, dark nights away from city lights.

Whale Watching

Popular activity from Reykjavik's harbor with opportunities to see minke whales, humpbacks, dolphins, and porpoises in their natural habitat.

Must Try Cuisine in Reykjavik

Icelandic Lamb

Tender and flavorful meat from free-roaming sheep that graze on Iceland's wild herbs and grasses, a staple of Icelandic cuisine.

Skyr

Traditional Icelandic dairy product similar to yogurt but thicker and richer, often served with berries or as a healthy snack.

Fermented Shark

Traditional Icelandic delicacy with a strong ammonia smell and taste, typically eaten in small cubes during Þorrablót festival.

Icelandic Fish Soup

Hearty soup made with fresh local fish, potatoes, and vegetables in a creamy broth, perfect for cold Icelandic days.

Best Time to Visit

Spring

April - May (0-10°C)

Melting snow and longer days. Good time to visit with fewer crowds and nature awakening from winter.

Summer

June - August (10-20°C)

Midnight sun season with 24-hour daylight. Peak tourist season with all attractions accessible and vibrant atmosphere.

Fall

September - October (5-12°C)

Beautiful autumn colors and Northern Lights begin to appear. Good balance of accessibility and fewer tourists.

Winter

November - March (-5-5°C)

Dark with short days but prime time for Northern Lights. Snow-covered landscapes and cozy atmosphere. Some roads may be closed.

Travel Tips & Cost Estimate

Travel Tips

  • Dress in Layers

    Icelandic weather is unpredictable. Wear waterproof and windproof outer layers with warm inner layers that can be adjusted.

  • Rent a Car

    Consider renting a 4WD vehicle for flexibility, especially in winter. Book well in advance as availability can be limited.

  • Daylight Hours

    Check sunrise and sunset times for your visit. In winter, daylight is limited while summer has nearly 24 hours of daylight.

  • Currency

    Iceland uses the Icelandic Króna. Credit cards are widely accepted but carry some cash for smaller purchases.
