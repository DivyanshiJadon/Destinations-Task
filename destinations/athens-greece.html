About Athens

Athens, the cradle of Western civilization, is a city where ancient history meets modern urban life. As the birthplace of democracy, philosophy, and many Olympic Games, Athens holds a special place in world history and culture.

The city is dominated by the Acropolis, a flat-topped rock crowned by the Parthenon temple, which stands as a symbol of ancient Greek civilization. Beyond this iconic landmark, Athens offers a wealth of archaeological sites, museums, and vibrant neighborhoods.

Modern Athens is a bustling metropolis with a lively street life, excellent cuisine, and a thriving arts scene. The city's recent transformation ahead of the 2004 Olympics has improved infrastructure and created pedestrian-friendly areas around ancient sites.

Whether you're marveling at ancient temples, exploring the Plaka district's charming streets, enjoying traditional Greek cuisine, or simply soaking in the Mediterranean atmosphere, Athens offers a fascinating journey through thousands of years of history.

Top Attractions in Athens

Acropolis

Ancient citadel overlooking Athens, home to the Parthenon, Erechtheion, and other significant ancient structures.

Parthenon

Iconic temple dedicated to the goddess Athena, a masterpiece of Doric architecture and symbol of ancient Greece.

Acropolis Museum

Modern museum housing artifacts from the Acropolis, with glass floors revealing archaeological excavations below.

Ancient Agora

Ancient marketplace and center of Athenian public life, featuring the well-preserved Temple of Hephaestus.

Plaka District

Charming historic neighborhood at the foot of the Acropolis with narrow streets, neoclassical architecture, and traditional tavernas.

National Archaeological Museum

One of the world's most important museums, housing artifacts from prehistoric to late antiquity.

Must Try Cuisine in Athens

Moussaka

Traditional casserole with layers of eggplant, minced meat, and béchamel sauce, baked until golden.

Souvlaki

Grilled meat skewers, typically pork or chicken, served with pita bread, tzatziki, and vegetables.

Greek Salad

Fresh salad with tomatoes, cucumbers, onions, feta cheese, olives, and olive oil, seasoned with oregano.

Baklava

Sweet pastry made of layers of phyllo filled with chopped nuts and sweetened with honey or syrup.

Best Time to Visit

Spring

March - May (15-25°C)

Pleasant weather with blooming wildflowers. Ideal for sightseeing and outdoor activities.

Summer

June - August (25-38°C)

Hot and dry with intense sun. Early morning or late afternoon sightseeing recommended. Peak tourist season.

Fall

September - November (18-25°C)

Mild temperatures and fewer crowds. Excellent time for visiting archaeological sites.

Winter

December - February (8-15°C)

Cool and rainy but rarely cold. Fewer tourists and lower prices. Some sites may have reduced hours.

Travel Tips & Cost Estimate

Travel Tips

  • Sun Protection

    Greek sun is intense, especially in summer. Wear sunscreen, a hat, and carry water when exploring archaeological sites.

  • Combo Tickets

    Buy a combo ticket for the Acropolis and other archaeological sites to save money and time.

  • Site Hours

    Many archaeological sites close early (around 3-4pm). Check opening hours in advance and plan accordingly.

  • Dining Times

    Greeks eat late, with dinner typically starting at 9pm or later. Lunch is usually between 2-4pm.
