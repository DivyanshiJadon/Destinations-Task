About Lisbon

Lisbon, the hilly capital of Portugal, is a city of seven hills, historic trams, and stunning coastal views. With its colorful tiled buildings, narrow cobblestone streets, and historic neighborhoods, Lisbon offers a perfect blend of old-world charm and modern energy.

The city's rich maritime history is evident in its monuments, museums, and architecture. From the Belém Tower and Jerónimos Monastery, symbols of Portugal's Age of Discovery, to the medieval Alfama district with its Fado music houses, Lisbon tells the story of Portugal's golden age.

Beyond its historical significance, Lisbon is a vibrant city with a thriving food scene, lively nightlife, and beautiful beaches nearby. The city's unique light, mild climate, and friendly atmosphere make it a year-round destination.

Whether you're riding the iconic yellow tram 28, exploring historic castles, listening to Fado in a traditional tavern, or enjoying pastéis de nata in a local café, Lisbon captivates with its beauty, history, and undeniable charm.

Top Attractions in Lisbon

Belém Tower

UNESCO World Heritage site and symbol of Portugal's Age of Discovery, guarding the entrance to Lisbon's harbor.

Jerónimos Monastery

Stunning monastery of the Order of Saint Jerome, a masterpiece of Manueline architecture and final resting place of Vasco da Gama.

Alfama District

Oldest district of Lisbon with narrow, winding streets, historic buildings, and traditional Fado music venues.

Tram 28

Iconic yellow tram that winds through Lisbon's most picturesque neighborhoods, offering a scenic tour of the city.

São Jorge Castle

Historic castle overlooking the city, offering panoramic views of Lisbon and the Tagus River.

Commerce Square

Grand plaza on the Tagus River with triumphal arch, statue of King José I, and numerous cafés and restaurants.

Must Try Cuisine in Lisbon

Pastéis de Nata

Famous Portuguese custard tarts with a flaky pastry crust, best enjoyed warm with a sprinkle of cinnamon.

Bacalhau

Salted cod, Portugal's national dish, prepared in countless ways from grilled to baked with cream and potatoes.

Caldo Verde

Traditional Portuguese soup made with kale, potatoes, and chorizo, often served with cornbread.

Ginjinha

Traditional Portuguese liqueur made from ginja berries, typically served in a chocolate cup.

Best Time to Visit

Spring

March - May (15-22°C)

Pleasant weather with blooming flowers. Ideal for exploring neighborhoods and outdoor activities.

Summer

June - August (20-30°C)

Warm and sunny with long days. Perfect for beach trips to nearby Cascais and Estoril.

Fall

September - November (15-22°C)

Mild temperatures and fewer crowds. Great time for sightseeing and enjoying outdoor dining.

Winter

December - February (8-15°C)

Mild winters with occasional rain. Fewer tourists and lower prices. Christmas markets add festive charm.

Travel Tips & Cost Estimate

Travel Tips

  • Hills

    Lisbon is very hilly. Wear comfortable shoes and be prepared for steep climbs in neighborhoods like Alfama and Bairro Alto.

  • Public Transport

    Use trams, buses, and the metro to get around. The Viva Viagem card offers unlimited travel for a set period.

  • Dining Times

    Lunch is typically 1-3pm, dinner starts late (8-9pm or later). Many restaurants close between lunch and dinner service.

  • Fado Music

    Experience traditional Fado music in restaurants in Alfama or Bairro Alto. Book in advance for popular venues.
